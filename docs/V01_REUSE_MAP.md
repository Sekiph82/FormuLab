## V01 additions that are not present in the audited foundation

The connector/data-exchange foundation does not itself provide supplier product catalogs, supplier portal sessions, document discovery/download, immutable acquired PDF binaries, SDS/TDS parsing, field-level extraction review, or a reference-formulation corpus. These are V01 additions. They should be implemented as supplier-specific capabilities and evidence-processing stages that feed existing document/material/Data Exchange authorities, not as replacements for them.

The acquisition and intelligence path must preserve original supplier identity, source URL, product/grade ambiguity, document revision, content hash, and per-field evidence. Extracted or normalized data is a proposal. SDS composition does not prove complete commercial composition; Section 3 concentration does not establish active matter; supplier formulation examples are not measured validation; and missing values remain unknown. Existing authorization, safety, regulatory, approval, laboratory, audit, and Data Exchange gates continue to govern their respective decisions.

### Schema-level V01 invariants

1. `material_documents` is the canonical metadata collection, but it currently neither stores PDF bytes nor represents extracted values.
2. Supplier products and grades are not identical to `RawMaterial`; commercial blends may resolve to multiple chemical components or remain partially disclosed/ambiguous.
3. A supplier document's identity, revision, market, language, and acquisition record must remain separate from the extraction proposals it supports.
4. Extraction status and approval status must remain separate: accepting a field proposal is not MaterialDocument verification, regulatory verification, claim support, or formula approval.
5. New metadata collections and blob paths require explicit policy/registry, backup/restore, migration and audit treatment. The existing formulation attachment path does not automatically cover supplier catalog documents.

## Implementation guardrails derived from this audit

1. Add provider/surface behavior through the existing connector contract and connection lifecycle; do not build a second generic connector or Data Exchange framework.
2. Route imported catalog rows through existing mappings, crosswalk review, Data Exchange preview, validation, and explicit commit.
3. Keep source documents and extracted field proposals linked to existing material-document and evidence authorities; never silently overwrite `RawMaterial`.
4. Before adding a document binary store or metadata collection, define its master-data/attachment ownership, backup/restore path, revision immutability, retention behavior, and compatibility tests.
5. Treat unavailable supplier access as an explicit scope gap and use authorized user-attended or manual-assisted workflows; do not bypass portal controls.

### V01 integration constraints from the generation audit

1. Keep reference-formulation retrieval/normalization separate from the existing deterministic synthesis engine until source identity, evidence class, applicability and precedence are explicitly modeled.
2. Add new information as immutable source-linked records and candidate evidence; preserve unknowns, partial disclosure, ranges, conditions and the original supplier wording.
3. Treat the existing safety, regulatory, compatibility, cost, inventory, manufacturing and ranking modules as authorities. Extend their input adapters only where a reviewed canonical field is supported; do not create parallel verdict engines.
4. The generation subprocess cannot directly invoke shared TypeScript authorities. Any proposed cross-runtime contract must be explicit, versioned and validated at its boundary.
5. Keep initial formula synthesis distinct from post-generation evaluation, ranking, prediction and saved-version optimization. Preserve the latter workflows' exact source/version fingerprints.

### Coverage gaps and rules for adding V01 records

1. Master JSON files are broadly included by filename, but that does not make a new collection usable: it must also be allow-listed in Rust, represented in the TypeScript collection union, assigned a policy area, included in the generated policy registry, and covered by schema/backup tests.
2. Binary blobs outside the explicitly walked formulation/session/formula roots are currently omitted. V01-01.011 and V01-10.007 must exercise a dedicated supplier-blob include/restore path; a metadata-only backup is insufficient.
3. The existing backup manifest lists schema versions from the first row only and the generic migration registry is empty. New V01 schemas need consistent per-record version behavior, detection of unsupported versions, actual migration entries only when required, and tested rollback to a verified pre-migration backup.
4. Never archive plaintext credentials, portal sessions, cookies, API keys or supplier authorization tokens. Archive only safe opaque references if their portability and restore semantics are defined; otherwise require re-authentication after restore.
5. Restore must preserve content hashes, source revisions, product/component mappings, extraction claims, reviewer decisions and formula lineage exactly. Derived indexes may be rebuilt; source evidence and decisions may not be discarded or silently retargeted.
6. The current collector omits `data/master/migration_journal.jsonl` because it includes only `.json` master files. Decide and test its backup/retention treatment before V01 migrations depend on that journal as durable evidence.

## V01 terminology and identity vocabulary

Use these terms consistently in schemas, adapter contracts, UI and exported records. An identity match is a traceable proposal unless an authorized human or an existing deterministic exact-ID rule establishes the relevant binding.

| Term | V01 meaning | Must remain distinct from |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier | An organization that supplies or markets a product to the FormuLab customer. The supplier may be the manufacturer, a distributor, or a brand owner; preserve the role stated by the source. | Provider software, portal operator, manufacturer identity or document issuer when those parties differ. |
| Provider | A FormuLab adapter/profile implementation that knows how to discover or acquire data from one or more declared supplier surfaces. | The supplier organization and the authentication service itself. |
| Surface | A specifically scoped access surface operated for supplier data, such as a public document finder, REST API, technical library or authenticated customer portal. Record its access mode, host, market, language, product-line scope and supported capabilities. | A provider adapter as a whole; one provider may support several surfaces with different access rules. |
| Commercial product | The supplier-marketed item as identified by its supplier, trade name and product/grade identifiers, in a market/language context. It may be a substance, solution, polymer, blend, or partially disclosed product. | A single canonical chemical or a `RawMaterial` record. |
| Grade | A supplier-designated variant/specification of a commercial product, identified by the supplier's own grade or quality designation and applicable scope. | An assumed universal chemical identity; two grades may differ in composition, properties, market or revision. |
| Trade name | The original supplier/brand product name exactly as stated in source evidence, with source, market and language retained. | A canonical chemical name, INCI name, synonym normalization or translated display label. |
| Supplier product code | A supplier-issued catalog, material, article or grade identifier, including the issuing organization and scope needed to interpret it. | A FormuLab material code, CAS/EC number or globally unique identifier unless an explicit crosswalk says so. |
| Canonical chemical | A normalized identity for a chemically defined substance or polymer identity supported by resolvable identifiers and evidence. It can represent a proposed match; it is not a claim that a commercial mixture is one pure chemical. | A trade name, commercial product, formulation ingredient label or undisclosed mixture remainder. |
| Component | A disclosed constituent of a commercial product or source formula, with its original wording, identity proposal, amount/range, basis, source location and disclosure status. | An assumption that the source disclosed the complete composition or that the component is independently sold as a supplier product. |
| Blend | A commercial product or formula containing multiple constituents, including solutions and partially disclosed mixtures. Represent known components and ranges plus explicit unknown/proprietary remainder where applicable. | A single-chemical canonical identity or a fully known composition when the document gives only partial disclosure. |
| Source formula | The formulation as represented in its original supplier document, retaining original line names, sequence, amounts/units/bases, footnotes, procedure and source locations. The source file and revision are immutable evidence. | A FormuLab production formulation, normalized corpus formula or measured/validated laboratory recipe. |
| Reference formula | A source-linked, reviewable formulation example derived from a supplier formulation or other eligible source. Its evidence class and commercial context remain explicit; it informs empirical reference intelligence but does not establish efficacy or validation. | A saved user formula, approved formula, laboratory result, validated claim or production instruction. |
| Normalized formula | A deterministic, versioned, source-linked representation of a source formula after explicit unit/name/role/identity normalization. Every normalized line must retain its original source value and mapping; unresolved lines remain unresolved. | An overwrite of the source formula, a new scientific fact, or an authorized canonical `RawMaterial` update. |
| Evidence claim | One source-bound assertion about a field or formula feature, carrying document/revision identity, page/section/quote or coordinates, original and normalized value/unit, method/version and uncertainty/confidence where available. | The document itself, a verified master-data value, a reviewer decision or a general unqualified truth. |
| Review decision | A named human's field- or record-scoped accept, reject or request-more-review action on a specific proposal/version, with timestamp and rationale. Decisions are immutable history; a later decision supersedes by a new action. | Import completion, parser confidence, document verification, safety/regulatory approval or formula approval. |

### Identity and normalization rules

1. Preserve supplier, manufacturer, distributor, brand owner and document issuer as separate roles when the evidence identifies different organizations.
2. Resolve external product codes through an explicit, source-scoped crosswalk. A name, CAS match alone, or fuzzy similarity may suggest candidates but cannot silently bind a commercial product or blend to one `RawMaterial`.
3. Keep source text, normalized value and identity proposal together. Normalization never deletes or rewrites the source value.
4. Treat region, language, product line, grade and document revision as identity context; matching a name across contexts does not prove equivalence.
5. A component amount/range is not active-matter concentration unless the document explicitly supports that interpretation. Unknown, proprietary, and undisclosed remain distinct states.

## V01 scientific evidence classes

Assign evidence class from what the source actually establishes, not from the parser's confidence or how useful the value would be. A record can contain multiple claims of different classes; do not promote an entire document or product to its strongest-looking class.

| Evidence class | Meaning and minimum context | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Source document fact | A faithfully transcribed statement or datum appearing in a specific immutable source document revision, with source location and original wording/value retained. | Truth beyond the source, current validity, complete disclosure, independent verification or applicability to another market/grade. |
| Supplier formulation evidence | A formula, recipe, example composition or procedure the supplier explicitly publishes for a named product/application. Preserve amounts, basis, sequence, conditions, product family and source location. | Reproducibility, general applicability, safety/compliance, efficacy, commercial release, customer acceptance or measured validation in FormuLab. |
| SDS composition evidence | A Section 3 substance/mixture declaration or disclosed constituent/range as stated in a specific SDS revision and regulatory market. Preserve CAS/EC, exact/ranged amount, basis, trade-secret/undisclosed state and section location. | Full commercial formulation, absence of components not listed, active matter, function, compatibility or performance. |
| Typical physical-property evidence | A supplier-reported typical or representative property (for example pH, density, viscosity or appearance), preserving test method, temperature, concentration, unit, basis and wording such as “typical”. | A guaranteed specification limit, batch-specific measured result, acceptance limit, or a value transferable to a different grade/condition. |
| Supplier recommendation | A supplier's explicit recommended use, dosage, application, storage or process guidance, scoped to the stated product/application and source revision. | A universal optimum, regulatory permission, validated efficacy, mandatory process setting or an automatically applicable formulation parameter. |
| Canonical identity proposal | A candidate mapping from a trade name/product code/component label to canonical chemical or existing material identity, with identifiers, source evidence, resolver method, alternatives and uncertainty. | A verified supplier-product identity, complete blend composition, authorized RawMaterial change or safety/regulatory verification. |
| Measured laboratory result | A value or outcome produced by a recorded FormuLab LaboratoryTrial/TestResult or other explicitly governed experiment, tied to formula version, sample, method, conditions, instrument/operator and units as applicable. | A generally validated claim, regulatory approval, reproducibility beyond its study design or a result for a different formula/sample/condition. |
| Prediction | A model-produced estimate tied to model/version, exact input formula/version, feature/source snapshot, output and uncertainty/limitations. Keep it visibly model-derived. | An observation, measurement, validation, causal explanation, safety determination or ground truth. |
| Validation | A governed conclusion that specified acceptance criteria were met by suitable, attributable experimental evidence for a defined formula/product/use context, reviewed by the required authorized person(s). Preserve criterion, scope, evidence and decision lineage. | A blanket guarantee outside its tested scope, a consequence of model confidence, literature mention, supplier example, SDS fact or automatic system/parser decision. |

### Evidence class assignment and transitions

1. Store source fact and interpreted claim separately when extraction or normalization adds meaning. The source quotation/value remains the evidence anchor.
2. One document may support source-document facts, SDS composition evidence, typical property evidence and supplier recommendations simultaneously; classify each field claim independently.
3. A supplier formulation stays supplier formulation evidence after normalization, indexing, similarity retrieval or use as a generator prior. Those operations do not upgrade its class.
4. A lab measurement remains a measured result until the applicable validation criteria and authorized review are completed. A prediction can prioritize an experiment but cannot substitute for its outcome.
5. Confidence describes extraction/match uncertainty, not evidence strength or regulatory approval. Keep these dimensions separate in storage and presentation.

## V01 no-fabrication invariants

These invariants apply across acquisition, parsing/OCR, identity resolution, normalization, indexing, UI, exports and generator integration. A missing or ambiguous result is a valid outcome and must remain visible as such.

| Situation | Required behavior | Forbidden inference |
|---|---|---|
| Missing, unreadable, unsupported or unscanned data | Store an explicit unknown/partial/unavailable/unscanned state with the reason and scope. Keep the original blank or missing value blank. | Replacing missing values with zero, a default, a likely value, or “not present”. |
| SDS Section 3 lists only selected hazardous/disclosable components | Preserve each disclosed constituent and stated exact amount/range, plus explicit undisclosed/proprietary remainder or disclosure limitation when applicable. | Treating unlisted components as absent, summing ranges into a fabricated complete composition, or representing Section 3 as the complete commercial formula. |
| SDS composition amount | Retain source wording, concentration bounds, unit, basis, role/context, section and revision. | Reinterpreting a concentration as active matter, use level, formula dosage or purity unless the source explicitly makes that claim. |
| Supplier reference formulation | Preserve source line, sequence, basis, application context, brand/product and exact source revision. Label it supplier reference evidence through parsing, normalization and ranking. | Calling it a validated or proven production recipe, lab result, efficacy result, product claim or safety approval. |
| SDS or other safety document | Preserve claims as evidence bound to the document's jurisdiction and revision; show unknown/partial identity and coverage honestly. | Treating an SDS as formulation-performance proof, a verified live safety verdict, complete ingredient disclosure or automatic regulatory approval. |
| Supplier function or role wording | Store exact source wording separately from any proposed normalized function/role and retain the mapping rule/version and alternatives. | Writing the supplier's wording directly as canonical chemistry/function truth or using a parser's guess as a verified material attribute. |
| Commercial product identity | Keep supplier product, grade, market and trade name separate from component chemical identities. Record candidate matches, identifiers, evidence and unresolved remainder. | Collapsing a solution, polymer, blend or proprietary product into one chemical solely from a similar name or one CAS/EC mention. |
| Physical-property value | Preserve typical/specification wording, conditions, method, temperature, concentration, unit and basis; if context is absent, mark it absent. | Converting “typical” to a specification limit, transferring values across grades/conditions, or filling unstated conditions from a default. |
| Product/source coverage | Report what surfaces, markets, languages, product lines, pages and items were actually checked; distinguish no result from inaccessible, unsupported or unscanned. | Claiming “all products”, full catalog coverage or absence of documents from a partial/failed scan. |
| Duplicate and changed document | Deduplicate bytes by hash where appropriate while preserving every independent source/acquisition provenance; preserve changed revision as a new immutable source record linked by supersession. | Dropping a supplier/market/source relationship because bytes match, or overwriting an older revision with a newer one. |
| Extraction confidence and identity score | Describe extraction or match uncertainty for the particular field/candidate and expose conflicting alternatives. | Interpreting confidence as scientific evidence strength, authorization, verification, approval or permission to write master data. |
| Master-data application | Keep each extraction as a source-linked proposal. Apply only after an explicitly authorized human reviews and accepts the specific field through the existing master-data authority; preserve old/new value and audit lineage. | Direct overwrite, bulk auto-accept, system/AI/parser approval, or silently changing a saved formula, safety, regulatory, lab or supplier identity record. |
| Generator and reference intelligence | Use only eligible, reviewed reference evidence under an explicit deterministic policy; preserve source class and source IDs in influence/explanation. Keep hard safety/regulatory/compatibility/manufacturing/availability gates above popularity. | Treating a frequency prior as chemistry truth, performance validation, compatibility proof or a reason to bypass an explicit constraint/hard gate. |

When a parser cannot distinguish among materially different interpretations, it must emit the alternatives and request field-level review or keep the value unresolved. It must not choose the most convenient value simply to complete a record or keep an autonomous scan moving.
